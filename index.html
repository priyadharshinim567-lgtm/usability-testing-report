Usability Findings

Key issues and insights identified

Finding 01 — Study Session Flow Is Difficult to Discover

Users may not immediately understand where study sessions should be created.

Finding 02 — Priority Controls Lack Visibility

Some users may overlook the priority control when adding a new task.

Finding 03 — Calendar Expectations

Users expect the calendar to provide a stronger overview of academic activities, including study blocks.

Finding 04 — Dashboard Is Easy to Understand

Participants generally understand the purpose of the main dashboard instantly.

Finding 05 — Task Completion Is Straightforward

Marking tasks as complete requires minimal effort and is intuitive.

Design Recommendations

Actionable steps to improve the experience

1. Study Session Discoverability

Problem

Study session scheduling is difficult to discover.

Recommendation

Make "Schedule Study Session" a clearly visible primary action alongside "Add Task".

Expected Benefit

Reduces discovery time and user hesitation.

2. Priority Visibility

Problem

Users overlook the priority dropdown during task creation.

Recommendation

Use visible priority pill-labels (Low, Med, High) instead of a hidden dropdown.

Expected Benefit

Faster task creation and clearer prioritization.

3. Calendar Expectations

Problem

The calendar lacks context and interactivity.

Recommendation

Allow users to click calendar dates to instantly view or schedule activities.

Expected Benefit

Aligns with mental models of calendar interactions.

4. Progress Visibility

Problem

Completed tasks disappear without a sense of accomplishment.

Recommendation

Add a subtle celebration animation and clear weekly progress bar update.

Expected Benefit

Increases user satisfaction and motivation.

Design Principles

Guidelines established from our findings

Visibility

Important actions should be easy to discover. (e.g. Study sessions)

Consistency

Similar actions should behave consistently across the application.

Recognition

Users should recognize available actions rather than remember them from hidden menus.

Feedback

The system should clearly communicate task completion.

Simplicity

Common actions should require minimal effort and clicks.

Reflection: What I Learned

Personal insights from the testing process

Conducting this usability study highlighted that usability testing reveals problems that may not be obvious during design. Watching users perform tasks can reveal interaction problems much more effectively than relying on assumptions.

  • Task completion time and errors provide useful quantitative signals.
  • Qualitative observations help explain why users struggle.
  • Not every usability issue has the same priority; severity matrices are crucial.

Most importantly, findings must be translated into specific design recommendations. Iterative testing is the cornerstone of improving UX.
